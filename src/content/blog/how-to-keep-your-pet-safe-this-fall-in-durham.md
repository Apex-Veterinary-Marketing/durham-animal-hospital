## Recognizing Fall Health Risks for Dogs and Cats

With the arrival of fall, pet owners should pay close attention to changes in their dog or cat’s behavior and environment. Some of the most common seasonal risks include fluctuating temperatures, increased rainfall, mold and fungus exposure, and the presence of holiday-related foods or decorations that could be harmful if ingested. Symptoms to watch for are sneezing or coughing due to allergens, changes in appetite or energy, limping after outdoor play on slick or wet leaves, and gastrointestinal upset after accidental ingestion of seasonal treats or outdoor mushrooms. Other warning signs include skin irritation from damp conditions, lethargy, and shivering, especially in short-haired or older pets. If your pet is spending more time outdoors, additional hazards such as fleas, ticks, and heartworm-carrying mosquitoes remain a concern during milder autumn days.

## Why Fall Presents Unique Health Hazards for Pets

The fall season in Durham brings a unique combination of cooler nights and lingering warm afternoons, which can be confusing for pets as they adjust to the changing weather. Dogs and cats may be more likely to develop respiratory issues as temperatures shift, especially if they are older or have pre-existing health conditions. Dampness from rain and decaying leaves can foster the growth of molds and fungi, leading to potential skin irritations or respiratory problems. The presence of acorns, mushrooms, and decorative plants like chrysanthemums and pumpkins poses an increased risk of accidental ingestion, which can result in digestive upset or more serious toxicity. Additionally, with holiday celebrations such as Halloween and Thanksgiving on the horizon, pets may encounter chocolate, candy, and fatty table scraps, all of which can be hazardous to their health.

Parasite prevention remains crucial throughout the fall, as fleas and ticks can remain active until the first hard frost. Heartworm risk may persist with ongoing mosquito activity, so maintaining preventive measures is important. Seasonal changes can also affect your pet’s nutritional needs and activity levels, making it essential to adjust their care routine accordingly.

## Home Care Tips to Keep Your Pet Safe This Fall

Taking proactive steps at home can make a significant difference in your pet’s comfort and well-being during the fall. Start by ensuring your home is free from common autumn hazards. Store all holiday treats, chocolates, and candies out of reach, and keep festive decorations and candles in pet-safe areas. When walking your dog, avoid letting them sniff or eat mushrooms, acorns, or unfamiliar plants, as these can cause serious digestive or neurological issues. After outdoor adventures, wipe your pet’s paws to remove mud, allergens, and possible toxins, helping prevent skin irritation or ingestion of harmful substances during grooming.

Adjust your pet’s exercise routine to account for shorter days and cooler weather. While it’s important to keep your dog or cat active, consider moving playtime indoors during heavy rains or on chilly evenings. For pets with arthritis or mobility issues, provide warm bedding and limit exposure to damp, cold surfaces. Monitor your pet’s food intake; with less outdoor activity, some pets may require fewer calories, while others may benefit from joint-supporting supplements. Always provide fresh water and watch for signs of dehydration, as indoor heating systems can dry out the air and your pet’s skin.

Continue your pet’s flea, tick, and heartworm prevention throughout the fall. Even as temperatures drop, parasites may still pose a risk until winter arrives. Our veterinary team can recommend the most effective and safe products tailored to your pet’s needs.

## When to Schedule a Veterinary Visit

Knowing when to seek veterinary care is essential for early intervention and peace of mind. If your pet develops persistent coughing, sneezing, or difficulty breathing, it’s a sign that they may be struggling with allergies or respiratory infection and should be evaluated by a veterinarian. Sudden changes in appetite, vomiting, diarrhea, or lethargy are also reasons to schedule an appointment. If you notice your pet limping, displaying signs of pain, or shivering even when indoors, these could indicate joint problems or the need for additional warmth and support.

Dogs and cats who have ingested potentially toxic substances—such as chocolate, xylitol-containing candies, mushrooms, or plants—require immediate attention. Reactions to insect bites, unexplained swelling, or skin lesions likewise warrant a prompt visit. Finally, if your pet has not received a recent wellness check or is due for vaccine boosters or parasite testing, fall is an excellent time to ensure their preventive care is up to date.
